The eastern railway branch follows a shallow valley whose agricultural settlements expanded gradually around stations constructed during the final decades of the nineteenth century.

Passenger schedules varied with seasonal demand, while freight movements depended on the availability of storage space at the junction, creating a system in which minor changes at one location could affect several neighboring communities. Surviving timetables provide only a partial account of these interactions.

An adequate description therefore requires reading the published schedules alongside maintenance records and local correspondence, materials that reveal the difference between the formal design of the network and its ordinary operation.
